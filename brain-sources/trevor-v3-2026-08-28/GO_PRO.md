# GO_PRO.md
## How To Upgrade A Good AI Brain Into A Professional-Grade Brain

Status: V2 operating playbook. V3 Brain release.

## The Idea

The Download creates a useful first draft.

Go Pro is the upgrade path.

Do not make people feel like their V1 was incomplete or wrong.

Say:

> This is already usable.
>
> Now we can make it more accurate, more powerful, and more like you.

## The Upgrade Layers

## Layer 0 - Deep Research Dig

Before asking the person for more material, search what already exists.

Potential sources, when available and authorized:

- Brain files and prior decisions
- conversation history and uploaded files
- Google Drive / Dropbox / cloud storage
- inbound and outbound email
- CRM records and support conversations
- call transcripts and meeting notes
- case studies and testimonials
- websites and blogs
- public social-media posts
- YouTube videos and transcripts
- podcasts and interviews
- search-engine results
- public book/product reviews
- books, articles, press, and published interviews
- analytics, ad data, content performance, and conversion data
- mentor / competitor public content for pattern research

For each useful finding capture:

- source
- URL or file reference when possible
- date
- exact claim / quote / fact
- whether it is current or historical
- confidence
- whether it conflicts with another source
- where it belongs in the Brain

Do not confuse finding more material with making the Brain better.

The job is to find **decision-grade gold**.


### Layer 1 - More Of Their Real Voice

Collect examples by FORMAT.

Do not throw everything into one undifferentiated pile.

Priority examples:

- best LinkedIn posts
- newsletters
- emails
- books
- articles
- video transcripts
- podcast transcripts
- sales pages
- VSLs
- ads
- DMs
- presentation transcripts
- stage talks
- client calls

For each sample, tag:

- format
- audience
- goal
- date
- performance if known
- "sounds like me" score, 1-10
- what to model
- what to avoid

## Layer 2 - Format-Specific Voice Modes

A person's voice is not one prompt.

Build modes for:

- copy
- articles
- headlines / hooks
- email / newsletter
- books
- social posts
- DMs
- video scripts
- sales calls
- presentations

Each mode should answer:

1. What job is this format doing?
2. How does this person naturally communicate here?
3. What proven structure should support it?
4. What does "bad" look like in this format?
5. What examples prove the rule?

## Layer 3 - Mentor Modeling

Ask directly:

> Who do you want to sound like?

> Who do you want to think like?

> Who do you want your marketing to work like?

> Who do you want your brand to look / feel like?

> Who do you want to become more like as an entrepreneur or leader?

These can be different people.

Separate:

- Personal / Business Role Models
- Marketing / Media Models
- Copy / Communication Models
- Visual / Creative Models
- Specialist Models by task
- Broad Zeus / board-of-directors list

Then research their public work for PRINCIPLES and PATTERNS.

Do not clone their exact prose.

Extract:

- hook patterns
- proof patterns
- story structure
- offer logic
- visual style
- pacing
- channel strategy
- CTA patterns
- teaching frameworks
- audience-building mechanisms

Then adapt the principle to the client's own facts and voice.

## Layer 4 - Proof Audit

Copy gets dramatically better when proof is organized.

Build a proof database with:

- names
- before
- after
- timeframe
- exact result
- what caused what
- source
- permission
- screenshot
- quote

Tag every claim:

- claimed
- documented
- verified
- audited
- approved for public use

## Layer 5 - Story Bank

Build a searchable Story Bank.

For every story:

- title
- when it happened
- people involved
- setup
- conflict
- stakes
- turning point
- result
- lesson
- exact phrases
- which audience problem it maps to
- which offer / framework it supports

Do not over-polish the raw story.

Keep the raw transcript too.

## Layer 6 - Signature Phrase Library

Extract recurring language from real speech.

Examples for Trevor:

- So...
- Here's the weird part.
- Here's the deal.
- One more thing...
- Lemme know.
- No biggie if not.
- Ask me how I know?
- This is a little embarrassing...

Build the library from evidence, not from AI stereotypes.

## Layer 7 - Anti-Voice Library

Examples the person hates are often as valuable as examples they love.

Collect:

- copy that sounds arrogant
- copy that sounds corporate
- jokes that feel forced
- phrases they would never say
- overlong paragraphs
- fake urgency
- fake inspiration
- AI filler

For each, explain why it fails.

## Layer 8 - Visual / Creative Brain

Voice is not only words.

Collect:

- websites they love
- thumbnails
- logos
- colors
- photography
- slides
- ads
- typography references
- video editing examples
- opening frames
- lower thirds
- caption treatment

Ask:

> What do you want to LOOK like?

> What should your content FEEL like before anyone reads a word?

## Layer 9 - Performance Data

When available, connect examples to actual results:

- views
- watch time
- clicks
- replies
- leads
- appointments
- revenue
- conversions
- comments
- shares

Do not assume the person's favorite piece is their most effective piece.

Separate:

- sounds most like me
- performs best
- best combination of both

## Layer 10 - CANI Loop

The Brain should improve from use.

After important content or campaigns:

1. What did we publish?
2. What happened?
3. What worked?
4. What failed?
5. What did the person like / hate?
6. What rule should change?
7. What example should enter the library?

Never delete the learning history.

Version it.

## Layer 11 - Inconsistency Audit

A Pro Brain should surface conflicts automatically.

Examples:

- two different prices
- two different audience definitions
- outdated titles or bios
- old offers that still appear online
- claimed results with no source
- a public promise that no longer matches delivery
- a mentor/style preference that conflicts with actual approved examples
- a goal that changed but old documents still present it as current

For each inconsistency, create:

**CONFLICT**

What disagrees.

**SOURCES**

Where each version came from.

**LIKELY CURRENT TRUTH**

Based on source priority and recency.

**NEEDS HUMAN DECISION?**

Yes / No.

Do not quietly "fix" contradictions. Surface them.

## The Pro Standard

A Pro Brain should be able to answer:

- Who is this person?
- Who are they for?
- What do they believe?
- What do they want?
- What do they sell?
- What do they refuse?
- What have they proven?
- How do they sound in each format?
- What stories do they tell?
- What should the AI never say?
- Who do they model and why?
- What is current vs historical?
- What is still undecided?
- What evidence supports each important claim?

If the Brain cannot answer one of those, add it to GAPS_AND_OPEN_LOOPS.md.
