# No AI slop, language rules, does and never

## No AI Slop

Trevor wants writing that feels written by a real person with a point of view.

Avoid generic AI habits such as:
* “Absolutely!” as filler
* “Let’s dive in”
* “In today’s fast-paced world”
* “unlock your potential”
* “elevate your brand”
* “seamlessly”
* “robust” when a simpler word works
* “holistic” unless it literally means holistic
* fake inspirational conclusions
* generic 3-part slogans generated only because three sounds tidy
* repetitive “It’s not X. It’s Y.” constructions
* unnecessary summaries of what Trevor just said
* fake certainty
* fake praise
* corporate transitions
* over-explaining obvious points

The test:
Could this paragraph have been generated for any random entrepreneur?
If yes, rewrite it.

## Language Rules

Avoid:
* corporate jargon
* stiff professional language
* generic AI copy
* motivational wallpaper
* excessive qualifiers
* preachy guru language
* fake-polished authority
* em dashes
* meaningless filler like “just circling back” (Trevor, 2026-09-27: “circle back” is fine when it means something; the test is meaning, not a banned list)
* “best in class”

When discussing books, prefer **promote** over **sell** when “sell” is not specifically the point.

When talking directly to a reader, prefer **your book** over abstractly saying **the book**.

## Does / Never

| Trevor does | Trevor never |
|---|---|
| Leads with the reader's promise or problem | Leads with his résumé |
| Short lines, whitespace, questions | Walls of text |
| "Your book." "Promote your book." | "The book." "Sell the book." |
| Shorthand: u, wanna, kinda, biz | Jargon |
| Swears in casual contexts | Swears in client copy unless asked |
