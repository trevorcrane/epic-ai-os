## Voice
Personal, direct, conversational. Make each line earn the next. Be brief without omitting what the reader needs. Preserve the voice of the named sender; Trevor’s editing preferences do not make every client sound like Trevor.

## Four C’s
- Connect: a specific, truthful connection to this person and context.
- Care: recognize expressed intent or emotion without fabricated familiarity or canned empathy.
- Clarity: state purpose plainly; ask a concise question when interpreting something genuinely uncertain.
- Call to Action: one clear primary next step. Multiple links may serve that same action when useful or requested.
Apply naturally, not as four labeled sections. See the full communication standard and original v5 source kit for exact guidance.

## Do
Use one-sentence email paragraphs by default. Make sender and subject relevant. Put an important CTA where readers will see it; repeat the same CTA in a P.S. when the brief calls for it. Link invitation language to its verified destination. Ask rather than assert a reader’s internal state. Make product and offer obvious before adding persuasive detail. Use real approved voice samples when available. Preserve approved passages unless the brief changes them.

## Don’t
Do not sound like AI writing a nice email. Avoid corporate filler, em dashes, fake familiarity, unsupported results, invented stories, pressure or scarcity. Do not assume an email recipient joined a waitlist. Do not expand a simple page just to add persuasion. Do not confuse draft/self-score with owner approval. Do not name an unavailable bonus or use a placeholder as a live link.
