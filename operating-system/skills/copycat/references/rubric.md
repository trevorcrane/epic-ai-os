# Copycat editorial evaluation
Scores are editorial estimates, not measured conversion probabilities. Use the same rubric across versions and explain changed scores with concrete textual improvements.

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Reader/arrival-context fit and immediate orientation | 20 |
| Offer and next-action clarity | 20 |
| Relevant, concrete value and persuasive strength | 20 |
| Truth, credibility and substantiated claims | 20 |
| Efficiency, readability, channel/mobile fit | 10 |
| Voice fidelity and human connection | 10 |

Report each dimension, total /100, largest weakness, targeted change, full rewrite and new score. Do not promise a result merely because copy scores well. Unknown audience performance remains unknown.

Hard blockers cannot be averaged away: false offer implication; invented proof, facts, scarcity or personal experience; unsupported health/financial claims; unverified fulfillment promises; broken primary action. Fix or label them before declaring the draft ready for review. Applicable independent-review rules remain in effect.

Cold-reader check: after five seconds, can a reader say what this is, who it is for, why it matters, what they get, and what to do? For traffic sent a specific invitation, prioritize continuity with that promise. For truly unaware traffic, a problem-led opening may be appropriate. Do not universalize the Leigh example to every campaign.

If an honest 95 is unreachable, identify the missing evidence or unresolved tradeoff. Keep the best version and continue unaffected work. Never inflate scores to obey the threshold.
