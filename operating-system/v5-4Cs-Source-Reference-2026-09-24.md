# The 4 C's: Core Framework
v5 | Updated 9/24/26 | Created by Trevor Crane | Powered by epic.media

## The goal, every time

They feel **seen**. They feel **heard**. They feel **understood**. They feel **confident** it's being taken care of.

## The 4 C's, in order

### 1. Connect
Get their attention first. Use their name. Reference what they said, what they posted, or why you're here. Make it obvious this message is for them.

Example: "Hey Sarah, saw you just hit 10 years in your field. That's a real milestone."

Result: they feel **SEEN**.

### 2. Care
Acknowledge the emotion or intent behind what they said, not just the words. Warmth first, logistics second. You don't have to agree with someone to acknowledge how they feel.

Example: "I totally get it. That's a real frustration, and you're not alone in feeling it."

Result: they feel **HEARD**.

### 3. Clarity
State the purpose simply and confirm what THEY want. Ask it as a question, not a conclusion. Clarity makes them feel smart, and a person who feels smart feels confident.

Example: "If I understand right, you're asking whether this works without running ads, is that it?"

Result: they feel **UNDERSTOOD**.

### 4. Call to Action
One obvious next step. An invitation, not a push. Ideally something they do, so they're in control of their own outcome.

Example: "Would you be completely against a quick 15 min chat? Totally your call."

Result: they feel **CONFIDENT**.

Skip one and you create a gap. A gap becomes doubt. Doubt becomes "let me think about it." And a confused mind says no and does nothing.

## Goal #2: teach them to find their own answer
Some questions we should just answer. Some questions the person can find themselves. When it's the second kind, our job is to point, not to become the place they always come for answers.

Think of it like teaching a kid to look up a word in the dictionary instead of handing them the definition every time. We're not being less helpful. We're making them more resourceful.

## Speed first
Speed matters more than having the full answer. A fast reply that says "got it, I'm on it" beats a slow reply that has everything figured out. Target: five minutes or less.

Never promise to fix something by a specific time unless you know you'll hit it.

"Got it, thank you for flagging this. I'm looking into it now and I'll get back to you as soon as I have an answer."

## Clarity is a question, not a conclusion
Right: "If I understand correctly, you're saying you paid in full, is that right?"

Wrong: "If I understand correctly, you paid in full." That states it as fact before anyone has checked. If it turns out to be wrong, you've now argued with a client in writing.

Confirm their version of the story. Don't confirm the outcome until you actually know it.

## "I'm not sure" is a great answer
Telling the truth builds trust faster than pretending to know. Our job was never to always have the answer. It's to show people where to find it, or to go find it and come back.

## Deliver confidence
This is the energy under the Call to Action. From Trevor's training, Epic Influence: Deliver Confidence (vimeo.com/519592367):

"The most confident person in a conversation is going to lead. The person with the most certainty influences the rest. Your job is to deliver that confidence, not for you, but for your client."

If you want sushi and they want tacos more than you want sushi, you're going to tacos. Whoever holds the most certainty sets the direction.

So before a hard reply, get certain first. Not certain about the outcome, certain that you've got this and it's going to be handled. If you catch yourself playing small or hedging, stop. Take a breath. Stand up. And serve.

# The 4 C's: Always and Never
v5 | Updated 9/24/26 | Created by Trevor Crane | Powered by epic.media

## Always
- Respond fast. Five minutes or less is the target. Turn on notifications on every device.
- Match and mirror. Short message in, short message out. Mirror their tone, language, and formatting.
- Keep it short. Several short human messages beat one long perfect one.
- Give one next step. Not three options, not a menu.
- Be specific when you point. "Module 2, lesson 3" beats "it's in the course somewhere."
- Say "I'm not sure" when you're not sure. Then say where you'll find out.
- Close the loop. When it's resolved, post the update in the same thread.
- Write in the voice of whoever you represent. The reader should never guess someone else wrote it.
- Include the timezone on every appointment. Thursday, Oct 1 at 1:00 PM ET.
- Compliment genuinely and specifically. Generic compliments backfire.
- Expect 2 to 5 drafts with AI before it sounds human. That's normal.

## Never
- Never use em dashes. Not in DMs, emails, docs, prompts, or code comments. It's the fastest AI tell there is.
- Never promise a deadline you can't guarantee. A missed deadline is a second broken promise.
- Never state a conclusion as fact before you've checked. Confirm their version as a question.
- Never say "I'll handle it" about something you don't own. Acknowledge, clarify, point.
- Never end a findable answer with "let me know if you can't find it." It trains them to come back and ask again.
- Never resolve money, refunds, or contracts unless you hold the Client Success role.
- Never drop the calendar link in every message. Reference it, ask if they saw it, or re-send only when it fits.
- Never send the same message twice. Personalize every one.
- Never send walls of text. People read on their phones.
- Never use corporate filler: "I hope this finds you well," "Certainly!", "circle back," "leverage."
- Never copy and paste AI output without rewriting it in a human voice.
- Never argue, shame, or guilt. Truth with care, every time.

## Sounding human
- Casual beats professional. Personalized beats polished.
- A little imperfect is fine. Perfectly punctuated, perfectly structured messages read as AI.
- Short lines, white space, one thought per line.
- With AI, the first draft is rarely the one you send. Go back and forth 2 to 5 times. That's the process working, not the prompt failing.
- The test: would you actually say this out loud to someone you care about? If not, rewrite it.

# The 4 C's: Call to Action and Pointers
v5 | Updated 9/24/26 | Created by Trevor Crane | Powered by epic.media

## Call to Action variations
Pick the one that's actually true. Never fake certainty.

- **You know the fix:** "I'll make sure this gets fixed and confirm it back to you."
- **You don't know yet:** "I don't have the answer yet, but I'm going to find out and get back to you as soon as I do."
- **It's their move:** "The fastest way to get this moving is [specific action]. Once you do that, here's what happens next."
- **It's a real unknown:** "Honestly, I'm not sure yet what the fix looks like. But I've got it, and I'll follow up the moment I know."
- **It needs escalation:** "I'm bringing this to our Client Success team right now so you get the right answer fast."
- **Cold outreach:** "Would you be completely against a quick chat? Totally your call."
- **Booking a call:** "Want me to send a couple of times, or grab one here: [link]"
- **Checking understanding:** "Does that answer it?"

## Where to point people (beyond the 4 C's)
When the answer already exists, point. Sound celebratory, never dismissive. Be specific.

- **5. Content:** A specific module, video, doc, or link. Say: "Oh, this one's easy! It's right here: [exact link]. That's exactly why we put it there."
- **6. Coaching call:** They submit the question and get it answered live. Say: "Great one for the live call. Submit it here: [link] and [coach] can answer it live."
- **7. Community:** The group, WhatsApp, Facebook, wherever your people are. Say: "Drop it in the group, someone's probably already asked this exact thing!"
- **8. AI assistant:** Your branded AI (for example, RobynAI). Say: "Ask [AI assistant] at [link]. It can usually answer this in seconds, any time of day."

Don't end a findable answer with "let me know if you still can't find it." Point them somewhere real and let that be the answer.

# The 4 C's: Variations by Situation
v5 | Updated 9/24/26 | Created by Trevor Crane | Powered by epic.media

## Cold outreach: Compliment, Cause, Call to Action
When reaching out to someone who doesn't know you yet (legends, leaders, promotional partners, interview guests), this can replace or front-load the first two C's. It's a strategy inside the 4 C's, not a separate framework, and how you use it depends on the situation.

- **Compliment:** specific and genuine. Something that couldn't be sent to anyone else.
- **Cause:** a mission, value, project, or connection you share. A reason that isn't just "I want something from you."
- **Call to Action:** one small ask. An interview, a chat, a reply.

The goal of a DM sequence can simply be to start a conversation. It doesn't always have to book an appointment. Triggering a chat first often makes more sense than dropping a calendar link, though sometimes the link is exactly right. The appointment often follows the chat.

A sequence that often works:
1. Connect and invite. Short, specific, human. Usually no link.
2. A friendly bump a few days later. "Popping this back up in case it got buried."
3. A pattern interrupt. "Who schedules your calls?" or "Have you given up on your book?"

Don't paste the calendar link into every message. Reference it, ask if they saw it, or re-send it when it fits.

## Client care: point, don't promise
The Karen Johnson role (see 05-AI-Agents) exists because a past team member said "I'll handle it" to everything and got buried. The fix: acknowledge, clarify, and point. The Call to Action points to Content, a Coaching call, the Community, the AI assistant, or a human (Client Success). Nobody commits to personally carrying something they don't own.

## Complaints, refunds, and money
This is the moment people remember. This is our chance to be Batman in a business suit. Handled well, a complaint turns into one of our happiest, most loyal clients.

That doesn't mean giving people whatever they ask for. It means running the 4 C's, fast.

Step one, always, right away:
"Got it, thank you for letting us know, and I'm sorry this happened. I hear you. I'm looking into this right now and I'll get back to you as soon as I have an answer."

Money, billing, refunds, and contracts are never resolved by AI, admin, or ops. They go to whoever holds the Client Success role (currently Trevor Crane). That's about authorization, not rank.

Don't just forward a problem. Hand over everything needed to solve it:

```
HANDOFF TO CLIENT SUCCESS
Who: [name] ([client / lead / prospect])
Channel: [email / WhatsApp / DM / GHL]
Issue, in plain language:
What they want:
What I already told them:
Links, screenshots, account details:
History (has this come up before?):
My read on the next step:
```

## Your team (Slack and internal)
1. Acknowledge: "Got it. Looking into this now."
2. Clarify in one reply: "Quick question: [only if needed]"
3. Plan: "I'll deliver [what] by [when]." Only name a time you'll hit.
4. Close the loop: post the result in the same thread. "Got it" isn't the end of the job.

Example routing (Epic and Robyn setup, adapt for any brand):
- #_h_support-inbox: all inbound client and lead messages. Human plus AI team (Hermes) watches.
- #_h_request-queue: Robyn or Sam requesting work. Clear requests only, no "ASAP."
- #_h_red-flag: true emergencies only. Money at risk, a client about to leave, a system down affecting revenue. When in doubt, it's not a red flag.

## Interviews and sales calls
The 4 C's run the whole call.
1. Set the stage: Connect, Care, and Clarity on why you're both here and what happens on the call.
2. Find and magnify the gap: where they are, where they want to be, what it's costing them. Let them do the math (how much, how long, how many).
3. Reflect, clarify, move: after every answer, reflect it back, confirm it, then move forward.
4. BAMFAM (Book A Meeting From A Meeting): the Call to Action is the next meeting, booked before you hang up.

# The 4 C's: Use Case Library
v5 | Updated 9/24/26 | Created by Trevor Crane | Powered by epic.media

Real situations, the message most people send, and the 4 C's version.

### 1. Cold outreach: interview invite
*Outreach.* You want to interview someone who doesn't know you yet.

**Instead of:**
> Hi, I help people write books. Would you be interested in a call?

**Try:**
> Hey Sarah, just saw you hit 10 years in your field. That's a real milestone, most people don't make it that far.
>
> I'm interviewing people who've built something real for my new book. You came to mind right away.
>
> Would you be completely against a quick interview? Totally your call.

**Why it works:** A specific compliment handles Connect and Care. Clarity on the ask and why her. A small, no-pressure CTA. No calendar link yet.

### 2. Podcast guest invite
*Outreach.* You want someone on your show.

**Instead of:**
> Hi, I host a podcast about entrepreneurship. Would you like to be a guest?

**Try:**
> Hey Marcus, your post on [topic] was one of the most honest takes I've seen on it.
>
> I host a show where I talk with people who've actually done the thing, not just talked about it. You qualify.
>
> Open to coming on? No prep needed. Just a conversation.

**Why it works:** Compliment, Cause, Call to Action. The cause (the show) gives him a reason that isn't just about you.

### 3. Lead says yes: move to a call
*Outreach.* A lead replies: "Sounds interesting, tell me more."

**Instead of:**
> Great! Here's my calendar: [link]. Also check out [link] and [link].

**Try:**
> Love that, Jen.
>
> Short version: we help coaches use a book to get clients before it's even written. Sounds like that's on your radar?
>
> Easiest next step is a quick 15 min call. Want me to send a couple of times, or grab one here: [link]

**Why it works:** One link, once. Clarity checks her interest as a question. One move with two easy ways to take it.

### 4. Lead went quiet (message 3)
*Outreach.* Two earlier messages, no reply. The link was already sent.

**Instead of:**
> Just following up again! Here's my calendar link again: [link]

**Try:**
> Hey Tom, who schedules your calls?

**Why it works:** A short pattern interrupt that needs a human answer. It doesn't spam the link.

### 5. A non-client asks for free help
*Outreach.* Someone says money is tight and asks for help getting to your event.

**Instead of:**
> Sorry, that's only for paying clients.

**Try:**
> Chris, I love this. Chasing two goals at once says a lot about you.
>
> So you're working to get to the event next month and hit that bonus, and you want the money-making activities to get there, right?
>
> Best place to start: [free resource link]. Go through it this week and tell me what you implement first.

**Why it works:** Generous and warm, points to a real free resource, and the CTA is something he does. Check with the owner before offering anything custom.

### 6. Client asks something they can find
*Clients.* "When's our next call?"

**Instead of:**
> It's Tuesday at 2pm! Let me know if you ever can't find it and I'll pull it up for you.

**Try:**
> Hey Dana! Tuesday at 2pm ET.
>
> Easy way to always find it: every call is listed here: [link]. That's exactly why we put it there.
>
> Any other questions, drop them in the group or submit one for the coaching call.

**Why it works:** Answers fast, then points to where to find it next time. No open door back to you.

### 7. Wrong emails going out
*Clients.* A new client keeps getting emails meant for an earlier cohort.

**Instead of:**
> Can you send a screenshot? We'll check.

**Try:**
> Hey Douglas, thanks for flagging this, and sorry, that's annoying to keep getting emails that aren't for you.
>
> So you started with the August group, and the emails you're getting are for the June class, right?
>
> I'm getting this fixed on our end. You can ignore the June emails for now, and I'll let you know once it's sorted.

**Why it works:** Gives him something to do right now, so he feels in control. Then close the loop when it's fixed.

### 8. You don't know the answer
*Clients.* A client asks something you genuinely don't know.

**Instead of:**
> I think it's probably fine?

**Try:**
> Great question, honestly I'm not totally sure! I'll find out and get back to you.
>
> In the meantime, you can submit it for the coaching call or ask [AI assistant], it might already be covered.

**Why it works:** "I'm not sure" is a great answer. The truth builds trust faster than a guess.

### 9. A prospect asks the price
*Clients.* "How much does it cost?"

**Instead of:**
> $30,000.

**Try:**
> Totally fair question, Lisa. It depends on what you need. We have everything from a free resource to fully done-for-you.
>
> So I point you to the right one: are you starting a book from scratch, or do you already have something written?

**Why it works:** Care for a smart question, then ask the question behind the question. The person asking the questions leads the conversation.

### 10. Billing mix-up
*Complaints and money.* A client got a payment reminder but says she paid in full.

**Instead of:**
> We'll look into it.

**Try:**
> Hi Rosemary, thank you for letting us know, and sorry for the confusion. That's frustrating to get a reminder if you've already paid.
>
> If I understand correctly, you're saying you paid in full, is that right?
>
> I'm looking into this now and I'll follow up as soon as I know more.

**Why it works:** Clarity is a question, not a conclusion. No deadline promised. Then it goes to Client Success with the full picture.

### 11. Refund request
*Complaints and money.* "This isn't what I expected. I want a refund."

**Instead of:**
> Sorry, our policy is no refunds.  (or)  Sure, I'll process that for you.

**Try:**
> Hey Mike, I really appreciate you telling me straight. I hear you, and I'm sorry this hasn't felt like what you expected.
>
> If I understand right, it feels like the program isn't delivering what you signed up for, is that fair?
>
> I'm bringing this to our Client Success team right now so you get a real answer. You'll hear back as soon as possible.

**Why it works:** Only Client Success decides money. Acknowledge fast, confirm their view as a question, hand off with the full picture.

### 12. Upset about a missed bonus session
*Complaints and money.* Clients say a bonus session was on their calendar today. It's actually on the 28th.

**Instead of:**
> That's not today, it's the 28th.

**Try:**
> Hey Darice, totally get why that's confusing. It showed up on your calendar for today, and that's on us.
>
> Just to confirm, you're asking about the GPT Accelerator bonus session, right? That one is August 28th, and we're fixing the calendar.
>
> You'll get the link before the session. So glad you're excited for it!

**Why it works:** Owns the confusion without blame, clarifies what they're really asking, and ends with confident, celebratory energy.

### 13. Internal Slack request
*Team.* A teammate posts a request in the team channel.

**Instead of:**
> ok

**Try:**
> Got it. Looking into this now.
>
> Quick question: is this for the August cohort only?
>
> Plan: fix goes in by tomorrow. I'll post here when it's done.

**Why it works:** Acknowledge, clarify in one reply, share the plan, and close the loop in the same thread.

### 14. Zoom Q&A chat
*Team.* Someone asks a question in the chat during a live training.

**Instead of:**
> Yes.

**Try:**
> Hey Bob, great question. What you're really asking is whether the training covers X, and yeah, we go deep on that in module 3. You're in the right place.

**Why it works:** Uses his name, reflects the real question, and points to the exact place. Fast and warm in one line.

### 15. Your spouse asks about dinner
*Life.* "What do you want for dinner?"

**Instead of:**
> I don't know, what do you want?

**Try:**
> Hey babe. Yes. Cheesecake Factory sounds perfect. Let's go.

**Why it works:** Clear, confident, one move. The 4 C's work at home too.

### 16. Your kid asks for something
*Life.* Your son texts asking if he can do something.

**Instead of:**
> No.

**Try:**
> Maverick. Yeah, I hear you. Here's the deal, finish your homework first, then we'll handle it together.

**Why it works:** He feels heard, knows exactly what to do next, and nobody's fighting.
