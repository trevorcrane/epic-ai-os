# Personal before professional
Version 1.2.0 | Owner correction: September 24, 2026
Scope: all Epic communication and Cat/Copycat work for Trevor and clients.

## The standard
Write as one person speaking to one person they care about. Imagine a real recipient across the table, not a segment of two thousand contacts. The reader should feel understood and spoken with, not marketed at.

Trevor's instruction is personal over professional. Professional means accurate, considerate and respectful; it does not mean stiff, immaculate or distant. Care applies across brands. Trevor's profanity, slang and personal stories do not automatically become a client's voice.

## Apply it
- Use the named sender's real vocabulary, natural contractions and approved samples.
- Start with what matters to this reader now. Cut the throat-clearing, abstract emotional speeches and generic launch excitement.
- Prefer short, one-sentence paragraphs for email. Vary sentence length when it helps a real thought land; do not manufacture choppiness or mistakes to look human.
- Let warmth come from useful specifics and honest attention. Never invent a shared history, memory, symptom, purchase, signup or intimate relationship.
- Ask about possible experiences rather than announcing how a reader feels.
- Keep a sign-off and sender name on separate rendered lines. Do not let Markdown collapse them into one line.
- Remove decorative polish, symmetrical slogans and stock phrases that the sender would not say out loud.
- Preserve the clear next step and truthful offer. Personal does not mean vague or careless.
- Respect an explicitly approved passage. Make necessary changes visible for review rather than silently replacing it.

## Review test
Read it aloud as if to one cared-for person. Would the sender say this? Does it sound like a conversation or a professional writer demonstrating technique? Could any sentence go unchanged into a thousand unrelated campaigns? Replace generic lines with true context or cut them.

Voice fidelity and human connection are a release gate, even when a total self-score looks high. Self-scoring remains an editorial judgment, never evidence of performance or publication permission.

## Client review presentation
Put Purpose/Strategy and proposed timing before the actual email. Show one selected subject, the preview and full copy. Keep deployment instructions in an internal view. Client approval and edit requests are inputs for revision; Trevor gives the final release. Do not expose internal aliases, raw merge syntax or alternate copy that has already been rejected.
