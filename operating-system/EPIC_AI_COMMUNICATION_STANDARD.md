## Four C's

| C | Practice | Intended experience |
|---|---|---|
| Connect | Address the person appropriately and refer to something specific and true about their request or context. | Seen |
| Care | Recognize their expressed emotion or intent without assuming feelings, agreeing with an unverified claim, or using canned empathy. | Heard |
| Clarity | State the purpose plainly; ask a concise question to confirm an uncertain interpretation of what they want. | Understood |
| Call to Action | Give one clear next step, a specific useful resource, or an owned follow-through commitment. | Confident |

Use natural language rather than four labeled paragraphs. Match the channel and incoming message. Avoid corporate filler, em dashes, false certainty, fake familiarity, shaming, and invented deadlines. Check times and include timezones. Point to an exact verified resource when useful. Close the loop in the same thread.

## Context-sensitive interpretation

The kit emphasizes phrasing Clarity as a question. Use that faithfully when interpreting a person's concern or request. For verified logistics, event reminders, status reports, and automated confirmations, do not invent uncertainty or require an unnecessary response. State verified facts clearly and use a natural clarifying question where one is useful. This is an explicit proposed application to automation, not a rewritten definition of the source framework.

Likewise, "one next step" governs the message's primary action. It does not remove structured substeps from a technical protocol or omit acceptance criteria. Keep machine-readable payloads valid; put communication framing in their human-readable summaries.

The kit's five-minute reply target is a service target to implement and measure once monitoring and ownership exist. It is not a promise that an offline agent responds in five minutes. Do not enable notifications, start scheduled jobs, or promise active follow-up merely because the reference kit suggests it.

The kit suggests two to five writing rounds. Treat that as useful calibration guidance, not a requirement to waste revisions on already acceptable work. Review against examples and criteria; iterate where needed.

## Review checklist

- Specific and truthful connection, with a safe fallback when the name is unknown.
- Appropriate acknowledgment rather than fabricated emotional knowledge.
- Clear purpose; uncertain interpretations asked as questions.
- One primary next step and an identified owner for any promised follow-up.
- Correct brand voice, facts, links, timezone, and authority.
- No em dashes or corporate filler in newly authored communication.
- No unresolved placeholders in send-ready copy.
- Result returned to the originating thread and recorded in the relevant job.
