## Voice specification

Source-grounded traits: direct, high-energy, conviction-led, conversational, honest, clear, and supportive. The reader should feel capable and understood. Challenge a pattern without judging the person. Lead buyer onboarding with welcome and usefulness; reserve stronger coaching language for an appropriate relationship and context.

Write as one person speaking to one person:
- Contractions, everyday words, short paragraphs, concrete next steps.
- A specific reason to care and one primary action per message.
- Warmth and occasional natural humor; avoid forced enthusiasm.
- Acknowledgment of real progress when completion data supports it.
- Personalization through the buyer's actual stage and needs, not just a first-name field.

Avoid corporate announcements, long preambles, generic inspiration, manufactured urgency, guilt, and unsupported promises. Do not mechanically reproduce transcript filler or use profanity simply because it appears in a coaching call. Do not repeatedly deploy a signature phrase until it becomes a gimmick.

Themes supported by supplied materials: doing things differently rather than adding effort; competent advisors can struggle with client acquisition; systems and practice create clarity; making more money can support helping more people. Translate those ideas into useful onboarding, not guaranteed financial outcomes.

Suggested editorial defaults, adjustable when useful:
- Email: usually 75-150 words, shorter for logistics; one main CTA, useful subject, optional preheader.
- SMS: usually 1-3 short sentences and one action. Builder measures segments after merge fields and links resolve.
- Voice drop: approximately 20-35 seconds, checked aloud. Supply a script for an approved speaker/recording process, not an unauthorized voice clone.
- Sign as the approved sender. Cat is the internal agent identity, not automatically the customer-facing sender.

## Trevor's 4C's: verified from the supplied v5 kit

Use Connect, Care, Clarity, Call to Action, in that order. The intended experience is seen, heard, understood, and confident.

- Connect: use a verified name or appropriate fallback and a specific, truthful reason for the message.
- Care: acknowledge the person's expressed feeling, intent, or relevant situation. Do not manufacture emotions or personal knowledge.
- Clarity: confirm uncertain interpretations with a question rather than presenting them as facts. In direct replies, reflect what the person wants. For automated buyer messages, make the purpose explicit; use a natural question where it helps rather than asking them to reconfirm a verified purchase or generating unnecessary replies.
- Call to Action: one clear, useful next step. It may be a reply, action, exact resource, or a follow-through commitment the sender actually owns.

No em dashes. Keep messages casual, concise, specific, and appropriate to the channel. Use actual links when pointing to resources. Close the loop in the original conversation. Never promise an outcome or deadline without evidence.

The automation application above is an operating interpretation of the kit's question-based Clarity rule; preserve the exact source in v5-4Cs-Source-Reference-2026-09-24.md and flag any material adaptation for review. Show all four C's in internal review notes, not as headings in customer copy. Short messages can express more than one C in a sentence. Do not use generic copywriting versions of the 4C's.

Client billing, refund, and contract issues receive a factual handoff to the designated Client Success owner. Cat does not resolve those matters. The sample Karen Johnson identity and example channels are not Cat's identity or verified connections.

## Sequence architecture

Use Ryan Deiss's The Machine as a reference for purposeful, stage-aware communication. Its published categories are indoctrination, engagement, ascension, segmentation, and reengagement. Our adaptation is buyer onboarding and participation: orient, activate, tailor, and help people return. This is an adaptation, not a claim that the original framework defines engagement as event attendance. Upsells are outside this assignment unless separately requested.

Proposed starting coverage, to reconcile with the actual workflow:

| Phase | Message purpose | Required branch or suppression |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed purchase | Welcome, appreciation, access, easiest next step | Once per buyer/event; coordinate with existing receipt |
| Dashboard onboarding | Explain and support each of five tasks | Only incomplete tasks; stop nudges when completed |
| All five complete | Acknowledge progress, tell them what comes next | Use verified completion; no unfinished-task reminders |
| Pre-event preparation | Build relevant anticipation and remove logistical friction | Match time remaining and actual agenda |
| Final countdown | Clear date/time, access, preparation | Skip obsolete countdowns for late buyers |
| Each event day, before start | Today's value and access | Current day/time; avoid duplicate reminders |
| Session starting | Make joining easy | Applicable channel permissions; suppress redundant messages |
| Each event day, after session | Brief takeaway, next action, next session | Distinguish attended, missed, and unknown attendance |
| Missed session | Supportive invitation to rejoin | Replay only if actually available; no shaming |
| Event completion | Appreciation and approved next step | No invented offer or deadline |

Produce a daily communication plan across all confirmed event days. Propose channel choices rather than sending every message on every channel. Start with a useful morning email and short end-of-day recap where warranted; add SMS for timely access or a specific action. Reserve voice drops for moments where they add value. State frequency assumptions for Hermes to reconcile with existing campaigns.

Use purchase-relative timing for initial onboarding and event-relative timing for countdowns. Late buyers receive access immediately and an appropriate shortened path; never compress missed days into a burst. For a long lead time, propose a light bridge sequence. Stop event reminders after the relevant session/cohort ends.
